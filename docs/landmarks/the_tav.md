# The Tav (Vasse Tavern)

## What it is

The town-centre pub on the north-east corner of Queen and Albert Streets. It's the old **Vasse Hotel**: a hotel has stood here since at least 1888. The present two-storey building dates from 1906 and was enlarged in 1902-08. Large parts were demolished and rebuilt in the 2002-03 shopping centre works. It closed in April 2019 and reopened in September 2020 as the **Vasse Tavern**. OSM now names it **"The Tav"**, which is what locals call it.

It's still open. "The Tav (Busselton Tavern)" in docs/landmarks.md is this building. The only other pub OSM calls a "Tavern" near Busselton is "The Tavern and Lounge Bar" on Bussell Highway at Abbey (115.2575), well west of the town and outside the build area, so it's left out.

Heritage: on the City of Busselton's local heritage list (inHerit "Vasse Hotel", Federation Free Classical). The parapet is original, with its cornice, panels, acroteria and **"Vasse Hotel" in bas-relief**. The verandah fabric is all replacement.

## Footprint

Measured from the Landgate aerial `out/refs/the_tav/aerial_z.jpg` (centre 115.34525,-33.65185, half 25 m, so 0.049 m/px), checked against the OSM outline (way 370736565, the "The Tav" node sits on it). About ±1 m.

- **Orientation:** the long side runs along Queen Street (bearing 166/346). The **front faces Queen Street, bearing about 256**. The south side is on Albert Street.
- **Middle (of the main blocks):** **115.345288, -33.651794**.
- **Size:** about 33 m along Queen Street by 14 m deep. A wider north end is 21 m deep. A 2.8 m awning runs along Queen Street, and a 3.6 m two-storey verandah down the Albert Street side.
- **Roof outline:**
  - pale grey-white hipped roofs: one block at the north end with its ridge across, and a run of smaller hips behind the Queen Street parapet
  - a blue-grey strip along the whole Queen Street front: the awning and a skillion behind the parapet
  - the same strip wraps round the splayed south-west corner and along Albert Street (the verandah roof)
- **Setback:** none. The awning reaches the kerb of Queen Street, which has a hedged median. The flat-roofed shopping centre adjoins on the east.

## Walls and roof

Hex values are approximate.

- **Walls:**
  - upper floor: white-painted brick `#ECE8DC` with screened double-hung windows
  - parapet: cream `#EEE2C2`, with pilasters and a cornice
  - ground floor: navy `#3B4763` with timber-framed glazing and doors `#6B4A32`
- **Centre bay:** a navy bay with an arched pediment over the entry (drawn square here), with VASSE HOTEL lettering in the parapet above it.
- **Height:** two storeys. Walls about 7.2 m, parapet to about 8.4 m, centre bay about 9 m.
- **Roof:** corrugated, pale grey `#D6D8D5`, hipped, low pitch (about 25°).
- **Awning:** flat, grey `#8F9599`, about 2.8 m deep at about 3.4 m, on slim navy posts at the kerb.
- **Albert Street verandah:** two storeys, navy posts and balustrade, blue-grey roof. In 2019 a bottle shop drive-through sign hung from it at the corner.
- **Signage:**
  - parapet: VASSE HOTEL in raised lettering
  - awning fascia: VASSE TAVERN (assumed)
  - all as plain lettering, no logos

## Grounds

- **Footpath:** concrete under the awning. White café tables and chairs, with blue bollards along the kerb.
- **Trees:** one street tree in front of the centre bay.
- **Neighbours:**
  - Mitchell Park and the Queen Street shops to the north and west
  - the shopping centre car park behind (east)
  - the Albert Street and Queen Street traffic lights at the corner

## What a driver sees

From Queen Street southbound, or from the Albert Street lights:

1. A long two-storey cream and white front with a parapet and pilasters.
2. A deep grey awning on navy posts with tables under it.
3. A navy ground floor and the raised centre bay.
4. At the corner, a dark two-storey verandah running down Albert Street.

## Unsure

- **Current signage.** OSM says "The Tav"; the 2023 operator branding says "The Vasse Tavern". The awning fascia here says VASSE TAVERN. Change it to THE TAV if a current photo shows that.
- **The Albert Street verandah.** It's only seen at the corner in a 2019 dashcam frame. Its full length, and whether it also runs a short way along Queen Street, are guesses from the aerial.
- **The arch.** The centre bay's arched top is drawn as a square raised bay (specs have no arches).
- **Storeys at the back.** The north block's back half may be single storey.
- **The address.** inHerit gives 38 Duchess Street (an old street name) on the corner of Queen and Duchess Streets. OSM calls the south street Albert Street.

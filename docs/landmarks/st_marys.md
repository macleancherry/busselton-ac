# St Mary's Anglican Church

## What it is

St Mary's Anglican Church, on the north-east corner of Queen St and Peel Tce, by the Queen St roundabout south of the Vasse River. The foundation stone went down on 4 March 1844, it opened on 11 April 1845 and was consecrated in 1848. It is called the oldest stone church in Western Australia. It is on the State Register with its graveyard.

- Rubble limestone nave, three bays, about 40 by 20 ft inside, walls 16 ft to the roof line
- Small chancel (about 14 by 12 ft inside) and a vestry at the east end
- Hammer-beam roof under timber shingles: sheoak shingles since 1989 (aluminium tiles from 1972 to 1989)
- Separate timber bell tower, 1902, about 10 m tall, square, with a pyramid shingle roof
- Graveyard with about thirty visible graves from the 1840s on: marble headstones about 1 m tall, some with wrought iron surrounds or ledger slabs, and a modern low wall with memorial plaques

## Footprint

Measured from the Landgate aerial `out/refs/st_marys/aerial.jpg` (centre 115.34560,-33.65275, half 45 m, so 0.088 m/px) with a labelled grid (`aerial_grid.jpg`). The church sits square to north, not to Queen St, which runs a little east of north here. Single storey, so about ±0.5 m. Trees hide the north side.

- **Orientation:** the west gable faces Queen St (bearing 270). The south side faces the lawn and Peel Tce.
- **Nave:** about 13.2 m long (east-west) by 6.8 m wide, roof 7.2 m wide with its eaves. Middle of the church at about **115.345603, -33.652773**.
- **Chancel:** a lower gable at the east end, about 4.9 m square, narrower than the nave.
- **Vestry:** a flat-roofed block south of the chancel, about 3.4 m by 5 m, standing out about 2.4 m past the nave's south wall. Three vents on its pale roof.
- **Bell tower:** south of the nave's west end, about 3.5 m from the south wall. Pyramid roof about 4.4 m square. Middle at about **115.345543, -33.652832**. Its long shadow in the aerial matches the 10 m height.
- **Porch:** in photos, a small gabled porch with a blue arched door. Not visible in the aerial; placed on the north side here.
- **Grounds:** red brick paving between the west gable and Queen St and along the south wall, a lawn south of the church with a round garden bed, big peppermints and tuarts to the east and south-east, and the graves among them.

## Walls and roof

From photos `c_seltongnangarra01..03` (south side and west gable), `c_11220105345725574` (west gable), `c_seltongnangarra05` (porch and vestry end), `c_seltongnangarra10` (bell tower) and `c_seltongnangarra13/20/24` (graves). Hex values are approximate.

- **Walls:** rough coursed rubble limestone, grey with golden-brown patches, average `#A3967D`. Eaves about 4.9 m.
- **Plinth and buttresses:** cream render `#E4DBC3`. A rendered plinth about 1.6 m tall runs round the building. Stepped buttresses, also rendered cream, at the corners and between the nave windows, about 3.3 m tall. Two deeper buttresses stand at the west corners.
- **Roof:** steep gables, about 48°, timber shingles weathered to dark grey `#707276`. The cornice and gutters are cream.
- **Gable ends:** coped parapets rise a little above the roof line, capped in cream. A small stone bellcote with an iron cross stands on the apex of the west gable.
- **West gable:** a big round-arched recess outlined in cream, with two small round-headed windows. A small geometric rose window sits high in the gable.
- **Nave sides:** three round-headed windows on each side, stained glass, with cream hood mouldings. Wikipedia says three on the north and two on the south.
- **Chancel:** three lancet windows in the east wall.
- **Vestry:** stone over the cream plinth, a cream coped parapet, a blue panelled door.
- **Bell tower:** an open frame of weathered grey timber posts and braces, splayed at the foot, with a steep shingle pyramid roof.
- **Sign:** "St Mary's Anglican Church" in cream on a maroon board with an arched top, on a low cream stone wall near the Queen St and Peel Tce corner (Mapillary `mly_2019-06_50m_look28`).

## What a driver sees

Coming north up Queen St from the Peel Tce roundabout, it is on the right:

1. The church sign on its low wall at the corner.
2. The tall grey shingle pyramid of the bell tower, in front of the church.
3. The west gable behind it: grey-gold stone, cream buttresses and plinth, the arched recess and the little bellcote and cross on top.
4. Dark trees on the lawn, with white headstones and crosses under them.

## How the spec draws it

`routes/landmarks/st_marys.json`, generated by the scratchpad `lm3/gen_stm.py`.

- Nave, chancel, vestry and porch are blocks. The parapets are stone pediments a little larger than each roof, so their rakes get the cream coping (trim).
- Plinth, buttresses, the recess outline, hood mouldings, doors on side walls, crosses and graves are panels.
- **Bell tower:** a dark core block under the pyramid, plus four grey corner posts and two rings of rails as panels. This reads as an open frame. The splayed legs and cross-braces aren't drawn.
- **Windows:** square-headed. specHouse has no arches.
- **Graves:** 28 low blocks south-east of the church: headstones, crosses on bases, ledger slabs. Panels, so no collision.

## Unsure

- **Where the porch is.** Photos show it, but trees hide it in the aerial. Here it is on the north side.
- **Where the bell tower is.** Wikipedia puts it in the north-west corner of the graveyard. The aerial shows a pyramid roof with a long shadow south-west of the nave, and the spec follows the aerial.
- **The graveyard's layout.** Records say south and west of the church; the trees in the aerial hide it. The graves here are spread south-east under the trees.
- The sign's exact place and which way it faces. It faces Queen St here; in life it faces the corner.
- Wall heights and pitches come from photos, not measurements.

# Bridgefield, Margaret River

## What it is

"New Bridgefield", the brick house on the corner at the north entrance to the shops, east side of Bussell Hwy (Main Street Historic Walk site 2):
- **1929-30:** built by Gavin Forrest for Elizabeth Higgins, whose family ran the 1896 Bridgefield halfway house by the river.
- **To 1989:** a boarding house, run by her daughter Agnes.
- **Later:** restored by Tom and Carmel Higgins.
- **Since 2003:** an Italian restaurant, La Scarpetta Trattoria, with accommodation units behind. Recent listings have Pizzica downstairs and La Scarpetta upstairs (Time Out gives Pizzica at 73 Bussell Hwy, a "rustic brick restaurant").

OSM has only the "Brigefield Guesthouse" point, no building.

## Footprint

Measured from the Landgate aerial `out/refs/mr_bridgefield/z.jpg` (centre 115.07448,-33.94648, half 15 m, so 0.0293 m/px; grid `zg.jpg`). About ±1 m: the roofs are a complex group, part under trees.

- **Orientation:** the house is turned about 14° off north. Its front faces the side street to the **north-north-west (346°)**. The pavilion stands on the west corner towards Bussell Hwy.
- **Middle of the main blocks:** **115.074463, -33.946475**.
- **House:** about 16.5 x 11 m under a red hip roof (solar panels on it), with a gable to the north.
- **Pavilion:** octagonal, about 4.7 m across, on the north-west corner.
- **Rear units:** a lower hipped block behind (outbuilding).

## Walls and roof

From a 2019 street photo (Mapillary 1253637068386543). Hex values are approximate.

- **Walls:** dark red-brown brick `#7E4B3B`. The north gable is filled with dark timber `#4E3427`.
- **Roof:** red `#8E3A35`, a tall chimney.
- **Pavilion:**
  - lower tier: dark timber posts with a cream frieze `#EFE6CF` under a red pointed roof
  - on top: a small cream lantern with little windows and its own red cap
- **Grounds:** a lawn with a white picket fence, hedges, palms and a tall palm by the corner.
- **Signs:** "La Scarpetta" in plain lettering on a free-standing board. No logo.

## What a driver sees

Just over the bridge, on the left behind the lawn:

1. The red two-tier pavilion on the corner, the first heritage building into town.
2. The brick house and its dark gable behind.

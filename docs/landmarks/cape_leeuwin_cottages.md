# Cape Leeuwin keepers' cottages

## What it is

The three lighthouse keepers' cottages of 1896, designed by George Temple Poole, in a row on the east side of the cape between the car park and the tower. The two southern cottages are the same limestone as the tower; the northern one is local granite/gneiss. Each had a detached stone laundry. One is now a museum. Later asbestos-cement garages, store and radio hut (1950s) stand round them.

## Footprint

From the Landgate aerial `out/refs/cape_leeuwin_cottages/lcott.jpg` (centre 115.13645,-34.3726, half 50 m), `out/refs/cape_leeuwin_cottages/leeu_wide.jpg` and OSM.

- **Three cottages** on a line running bearing about 175°, about 44 m apart. Each is under one pyramid roof about 17 m square that covers its verandahs; the walls inside are about 12.4 m square.
  - North (granite): 115.136414, -34.372203
  - Middle: **115.136452, -34.372606** (the spec's centre)
  - South: 115.136501, -34.373003
- **Laundries and tanks:** a small hipped laundry behind and to the north of each, and a round stone tank on the east side.
- **Drive:** the lighthouse road runs down the west side, a short driveway to each.
- **Facing:** the spec's front faces bearing **270°** (west, to the road).

## Walls and roof

- Limestone, cream `#E8E1CF`; the northern cottage grey-brown granite `#A59A8C`.
- Roofs: corrugated iron, light grey `#B9BCB8`, pyramid hips with two chimneys each.
- Verandahs all round under the roof edge.

## What a driver sees

At the end of the route, three square grey-roofed stone cottages in a row on the grass to the left of the road down to the lighthouse.

## Approximations

- The verandahs are the main roof's wide eaves (2.3 m) over the walls, without posts (specHouse's verandah is a single skillion on the front).
- The round tanks are short square blocks.
- The 1950s garages and sheds are left out.

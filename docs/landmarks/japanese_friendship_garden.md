# Japanese Friendship Garden

## What it is

The Busselton-Sugito Friendship Garden is a small Japanese garden beside the Busselton Library, marking the sister-city link with Sugito (since 1996). Five gardeners from Sugito built it with local volunteers in January-February 1998. It was realigned and redesigned in 2013 when the library was extended and the Community Resource Centre built.

It kept these features:

- the viewing platform (tenbodai)
- the water basin (tsukubai)
- large rocks
- stone lanterns (toro), including two large lanterns given by Sugito in 1999
- the gateway (mon)
- a dry watercourse
- benches made from old Busselton Jetty timber

An interpretive panel on the library wall tells its story.

## Footprint

Measured from the Landgate aerial `out/refs/parks/jfg_z.jpg` (centre 115.34745,-33.65105, 0.043 m/px).

- **Where:** the garden is the planted strip on the west side of the library, about 20 x 30 m, between the library's west wall and the car park road. It is almost all under maples (red-brown in the aerial) and paperbarks. The OSM area is drawn much bigger, over the whole library block.
- **The gate:** a small structure with a red-brown roof, about 3 x 2 m, at the south end of the garden where it meets the library forecourt. **115.347507, -33.651197**. Its roof runs about 78°/258°, square to the library. It faces the forecourt, bearing about 168°.
- **Lanterns and platform:** not visible under the trees. Drawn just inside the gate, a guess.

## Walls and roof

- **Gate (mon):**
  - two dark timber posts about 2.5 m high and 2.8 m apart, with a cross beam
  - a small gabled roof with eaves about 0.75 m out, in brown tile (`#76543e`, nearest roof: brown concrete tile)
  - **drawn with its doors shut**, because a spec block is always a solid wall from the ground
- **Lanterns:** grey stone (`#8f8b84`), about 2 m:
  - a square base
  - a post and a fire box
  - a wide hipped cap
- **Viewing platform:** a timber deck about 3.5 x 2.5 m.

## What a driver sees

Very little. From the car park road on the west side, the garden is a clump of maples and paperbarks against the library wall. From the forecourt on the south, the dark timber gate with its little tiled roof shows under the trees.

- Where the lanterns and platform are.

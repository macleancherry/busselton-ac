# The Busselton Museum (Old Butter Factory)

## What it is

The Busselton Historical Society's museum at 76 Peel Terrace, on the south side of the road, backing onto the Vasse River and estuary wetlands.

It was a butter factory, built by the State government from 1918 to replace the 1898 Fairlawn factory. It became a powdered milk factory and depot from 1960, and a museum from 1975. The boiler house dates from the 1930s and the smoke stack from 1918. The west end and upper level were extended before 1973.

It is on the State Register of Heritage Places (Place 03568, permanent entry 24 Apr 2003) and on the City's Heritage List (Category 1). Its style is "Federation Warehouse": brick and weatherboard walls with a corrugated iron roof.

## Footprint

Measured from the Landgate aerial (`out/refs/busselton_museum/aerial50.jpg`, 0.098 m/px) and checked against the OSM outline. OSM sits about 3 m north of the imagery, so I used the aerial. Roof edges were read at eave level; the low building leans very little.

- **Overall:** a near-rectangular cluster, **38 m along the front by 22 to 25 m deep**.
  - Front: 37.9 m, bearing 77°/257°, parallel to Peel Terrace.
  - West edge: 22.4 m. East edge: 25.1 m. Both run on a bearing of 166°.
  - The rear (south) edge is stepped. OSM area is 922 m².
- **Faces:** the front faces **NNW (bearing about 347°) onto Peel Terrace**.
- **Setback:** about **20 m** from the south kerb of Peel Terrace. In between are a grass verge and a front asphalt car park (one row of angle bays plus the aisle, with an ACROD bay with yellow hatching at the west end).
- **The front in three parts (west to east, measured along the front):**
  - **0 to 12.4 m, west wing:** single-storey brick. On the far west, a long narrow wing (about 5.5 m wide) runs 22 m back. It has a **hipped north end** and a ridge running N-S. Next to it is a lower skillion or verandah roof.
  - **12.4 to 22.0 m, main block:** **two storeys**, about 9.6 m wide and 9 to 10 m deep, with a **front gable facing the street**. Its ridge runs N-S.
  - **22.0 to 37.9 m, east wing:** single-storey brick with a gable roof. The ridge runs parallel to the street and there is a gable end on the east. A lower **hipped** wing sits at the north-east corner.
- **Behind (south):**
  - A tangle of low gable and skillion roofs: about 10 separate sections, with red-brown trim lines between them.
  - A **half-round (cylindrical) ridge ventilator**, about 4 m long, in the middle of the cluster.
  - A flat-roofed service court at the south-east.
- **Centre (lng,lat):**
  - Middle of the whole cluster: **115.349815,-33.652536**.
  - Middle of the two-storey block: 115.349784,-33.652485.
  - OSM centroid: 115.349834,-33.652510.
- **Boiler house and stack:** about 38 m behind the front, near the river. The stack base is at about 115.34997,-33.65285. It is not part of the main building, but tall.

## Walls and roof

- **Ground-floor walls:** red face brick (common brick, English-style bond), about `#8e4a3c`. Lintel panels over the windows and door heads are painted cream render (`#e3d6b4`). Sills are cream.
- **Upper storey of the main block:** cream-painted **weatherboard** (`#e8dfc4`). The gable triangle has **vertical board-and-batten** infill. Wall height:
  - ground floor about 4 m
  - upper storey about 3 m
  - eaves about 7 m, ridge about 9.5 m (estimated from the photo)
- **Roofs:** corrugated metal, light zincalume or galvanised grey (`#b8bfc0`). The trim is **dark red (Manor Red-like, about `#6e2a2e`)** on all bargeboards, fascias, gutters and the trim lines between roof sections. Pitches:
  - main gable about 30°
  - single-storey wing roofs about 25°
  - skillions about 10°
- **Front (NNW) face, left to right as seen from Peel Terrace:**
  - **West wing:** a lean-to verandah at the far left, then brick wall with **4 tall narrow sash windows** (about 0.9 x 1.8 m) and a door. Each has a cream lintel band.
  - **Main block ground floor:**
    - At its left (west) edge, the **museum entrance**: a white panelled door about 1.2 x 2.4 m, with a cream render surround and fanlight, a ramp and handrails.
    - Since about the 2020s, a **glazed entrance porch** encloses it: a box about 3.5 m wide, 2 m deep and 3 m high, with thin dark frames and a flat roof.
    - A small square window, then a vertical service hatch/door above the entrance.
  - **Main block upper storey:** two 6-pane sash windows (about 0.8 x 1.2 m), one each side of a central **loading door** (about 1.1 x 2.2 m, white boarded) under the gable.
  - **East wing:** a tall sash window, then a **white double door** (about 1.6 x 2.4 m) under a cream panel. Then a smaller 4-pane window at the hipped north-east wing, a mosaic wall panel, and a second small glazed porch near the east end.
- **Chimneys:** a small round metal flue with a conical cap on the east wing roof. The **smoke stack** is at the boiler house at the rear. In a recent drone photo it looks like a pale metal (white or galvanised) round stack, 12 to 18 m tall (estimate).
- **Signage (plain lettering only):**
  - "BUSSELTON MUSEUM" in dark capitals on the cream panel over the entrance door, about 2.2 m wide.
  - A white sign board on the brick wall left of the entrance: "Museum" with opening times.
  - Older photos show "GALLERY" over the double door.
  - A thin TV/radio mast on the main gable.

## Grounds

- **Front:** an asphalt car park with white bay lines and black-and-yellow hatching for the disabled bay. A strip of grass lies between it and Peel Terrace. Black bollards (red tops) stand along the porch fronts.
- **Sides:** a driveway runs down the west side to rear parking under trees (a group of large peppermint or eucalypt trees at the west). A sealed drive runs down the east side.
- **Rear (not visible from the road):**
  - lawns sloping to the Vasse River
  - several small relocated heritage buildings: a weatherboard school, a railway station building, group settlement huts and a machinery shed
  - old farm machinery on the grass
- **East neighbour:** a long gabled machinery-display shed and smaller huts on lawn, with a chain-mesh fence.

## What a driver sees

1. A **two-storey front gable** with cream weatherboard and a white loading door up top, sitting on a red-brick ground floor.
2. **Dark red trim** on every roof edge over pale grey corrugated roofs.
3. Long, low **red-brick wings** either side, with tall narrow windows and cream lintels.
4. A modern **glass entrance porch** in front of the main door.
5. A wide front car park and grass verge, with big trees to the west and the stack poking up behind (if tall enough).

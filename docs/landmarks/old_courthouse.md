# Old Courthouse complex (and the Old Post Office cafe)

## What it is

Busselton Court House and Police Complex, 4 Queen St, on the corner of Marine Tce. A linked group of brick and limestone buildings from 1860 to 1906:

- 1860 courtroom and bond store
- 1861 police office, cells, stables and police cottages
- 1873 post and telegraph office: two rooms facing Queen St, rendered limestone
- 1897 court house: red brick, designed in George Temple Poole's office
- 1906 police cottages

It has been on the State Register since 1993, and an arts and cultural centre since 1985. "The Old Post Office Cafe" (OSM) works out of the old post office rooms and the court house verandah. Its outdoor tables sit on the Queen St footpath.

**Name clash:** the **ArtGeo Gallery** building is not part of this group. It is the 1931 Department of Agriculture office (red tiled roof, rendered brick, three arches) at **7 Queen St, on the opposite (east) corner** of Queen St and Marine Tce. It is only mentioned here so it isn't confused with the courthouse.

## Footprint

Measured from the Landgate aerial `out/refs/old_courthouse/aerial_w.jpg` (centre 115.34330,-33.64722, half 40 m, so 0.078 m/px) with a labelled pixel grid. Lengths are along and across the building axes, which run at about 13° off north-south, square to Queen St. Single storey, so about ±0.5 m. Large trees hide the north-west corner.

- **Orientation:** the group fronts **east onto Queen St** (front faces bearing about 76°). The court house's north side faces Marine Tce across a treed lawn.
- **Main group (court house, post office, police office):** one linked block about 27 m along Queen St by 14-19 m deep.
- **1897 court house:** the north end of the block, about 15 m along Queen St by 14 m deep. Middle at **115.343496, -33.647078**.
  - Hip roof with a square ventilator lantern on the ridge.
  - A projecting gable bay on the street face.
  - A verandah to the left (south) of the gable bay, under the main roof.
- **Old post office (cafe):** directly south of the court house on the Queen St frontage, about 8 m along the street. Middle at about **115.343543, -33.647173**. Separate lower hip roof.
- **Police office and other rooms:** behind the post office, with hip roofs, small skylights and several brick chimneys.
- **Rear limestone range (cells, stables, bond store):** a long narrow building running west-south-west from the back of the group, about 37 m by 5 m. Gable roof with the ridge along its length. A darker lower roof (lean-to or covered way, about 4 m) runs along its south side.
- **Police cottages (1906):** a separate building south of the rear range, about 21 m along Queen St by 19 m deep. Middle at about **115.343484, -33.647374**. Two hip roofs (two semi-detached cottages) and a front verandah to Queen St.
- **Setback:** the court house front wall is about 5 m from the Queen St kerb. The footpath is wide and paved, and the cafe tables fill it. The cottages sit back about 8 m behind a widened paved area. The court house north wall is about 15 m back from Marine Tce, behind lawn and big trees.

## Walls and roof

From photos `rt_0` (court house and cafe from Queen St), `bcp_7` (historic photo, same face), `bcp_0` (1916 view), `bcp_5` (cottages), `bcpo_0` (limestone end), `wlpo_1` (cafe terrace) and `mly_0` (Queen St, 2021). Hex values are approximate.

- **Court house walls:** red-brown face brick `#8A4B3A`, with cream rendered sills, window heads and string course `#D9CDB0`. Single storey, eaves about 4.5 m. The ridge with its ventilator lantern is about 9 m.
- **Post office, cells and stables:** coursed limestone blocks, cream `#E2DCC8`. Some lower courses are whitewashed. Single storey, eaves about 3.5 m.
- **Police cottages:** red brick like the court house, cream trim.
- **Roofs:** corrugated zincalume, pale silver-grey `#C9CDCF` (reads `#DDDFD9` in the aerial). Hips at about 30°. Dark green gutters and fascias `#3E4F3A` with a cream eaves board `#D3B9A3`.
- **Court house gable bay:** projects about 1.5 m towards Queen St and is about 6 m wide. The gable has a half-timbered infill (dark timber over a cream panel), a dark green barge board and a small finial. Below it are a central panelled cream door and two tall timber sash windows (about 0.9 x 2.2 m), one each side, with cream heads and sills. A small dark plaque reading "COURT HOUSE" sits above the door.
- **Court house verandah:** about 8 m long and 2.5 m deep, under the main roof line.
  - posts: square, deep maroon `#6B2A2E` with cream banded collars `#E8DDBF`, about 2 m apart
  - balustrade: cream timber `#EDE6CF`, about 0.9 m tall
  - roll-down blinds hang between the posts
- **Street face, left to right as seen from Queen St:**
  - limestone post office wall (about 2 windows and a door)
  - the verandah, with the brick wall, a door and windows behind it
  - the gable bay (window, door, window)
  - then the north end of the court house turning the corner, with about 2 sash windows facing Marine Tce
- **Chimneys:** several brick chimneys rise through the roofs behind the court house. At least two are visible from Queen St in `mly_0`. The historic photo shows two tall corbelled chimneys on the court house.
- **Signage (plain lettering only):**
  - "OLD COURTHOUSE" in dark red capitals `#6A2A24`, about 0.4 m tall, on the limestone end wall that faces the forecourt between the main group and the cottages
  - "The Old Post Office Tearooms" on a pale banner on the verandah blind
  - "COFFEE HOUSE" on a dark board on the verandah fascia (`mly_0`)

## Grounds

- **Cafe terrace on the Queen St footpath:**
  - pale grey square pavers
  - timber folding tables and chairs
  - half-barrel planters with red flowers
  - black mesh windbreak screens about 1 m tall
  - large square brown or maroon market umbrellas
  - The aerial also shows blue-and-white umbrellas there.
- **Queen St itself:** dark asphalt with a band of decorative grey cobbles down the middle, and festoon lights strung across the street. Black heritage-style light poles.
- **North side (Marine Tce):** lawn and several large spreading shade trees between the court house and Marine Tce, with a red-brick paved path curving through the lawn.
- **Behind (west):** a small car park off the west side, garden beds, a lawn courtyard south of the rear range, and a bronze statue in the forecourt (`bcpo_0`).
- **Forecourt:** a paved plaza between the main group and the police cottages opens onto Queen St.
- **Fencing:** no front fence on Queen St today.

## What a driver sees

Driving south on Queen St from the Marine Tce roundabout, it is on the right:

1. Low red-brick and cream-limestone buildings with pale silver corrugated hip roofs and brick chimneys, close to the footpath.
2. The court house gable bay with a half-timbered gable, and the maroon-and-cream verandah beside it.
3. Cafe umbrellas, tables and black screens filling the footpath.
4. Big shade trees on the corner lawn at Marine Tce.
5. Further south, the brick police cottages with their own verandah, and the "OLD COURTHOUSE" lettering on the limestone wall across the plaza.

## Unsure

- The exact split between court house, post office and police office in the roof plan. Trees and roof glare in the aerial make the joins hard to read. The court house sizes are about ±1 m, not ±0.5 m.
- **Which café runs there now.** OSM says "The Old Post Office Cafe" and a photo shows "The Old Post Office Tearooms" on the blind, but other photos show umbrellas and screens for "The Lockup Coffee House". The building is the same either way.
- Whether the ventilator lantern is still full height. `rt_0` shows only its rendered square base.
- The use and walling of the darker roof along the south side of the rear range.
- Wall heights and roof pitches are estimated from photos, not measured.
- The aerial shows construction plant and orange barriers along the east side of Queen St, probably from building the new performing arts centre opposite. They may be gone now.

# Caves House Hotel, Yallingup

## What it is

The government hotel built for visitors to Yallingup Cave (Ngilgi Cave). The first Caves Accommodation House opened in January 1903. A fire in December 1930 destroyed most of the old wing. The present hotel was built in 1938-39 to a design by Principal Government Architect A. E. Clare, in the Inter-War Old English style, brick and tile, and the terraced gardens were laid out then. It is part of the State-registered Caves House Group and the Yallingup precinct (caves, Caves House, store, hall, power house). There's a plaque to Clarence Armstrong (drowned at Torpedo Rock in 1927) on the verandah wall facing the garden.

- **Around it:**
  - a red-brick-paved drive loops round the north-west and south-west sides
  - white marquees and a beer garden with blue umbrellas on the south-west side
  - car parks to the north-east and south
  - lawns and terraced gardens fall away to the north-west
- **Facing:** the spec's front faces bearing **130°** (south-east, towards the car parks and Caves Road, where the route passes).

## Walls and roof

- Walls: white-painted brick `#EEEADF` (assumed from memory of photos; inHerit only says brick).
- Gables: dark-stained half-timbering `#4A3428` over white infill.
- Roof: terracotta tiles `#B5583A`.
- Two storeys, eaves about 6.6 m, ridges about 12 m, chimneys about 13 m.
- Entrance: a porch with a dark canopy between the front gables, "CAVES HOUSE" in plain lettering.

## What a driver sees

The route passes 285 m east on Caves Road, through tall peppermint and marri, so from the drive it's glimpses of a big red-tiled roof and white gables through the trees by the Yallingup Beach Road turn. Close up, the white walls and half-timbered gables over the brick drive.

## Approximations

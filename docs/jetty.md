# Busselton Jetty precinct: reference facts for procedural modelling

Researched 2026-10-30. Labels: **[C]** confirmed by a cited source. **[OSM]** measured from current OSM data (Overpass, 30 Oct 2026). **[G]** guess or recollection, not confirmed: check against Landgate imagery, Mapillary or Mac's own photos before relying on it.

Online sources almost never give engineering dimensions (deck height, pile spacing, railing height). Those rows are marked [G]. The best way to settle them is a site visit with a tape measure, since Mac lives locally. A 10-minute walk would cover them: deck width, rail height, bay spacing, lamp spacing and lamp height.

| OSM railway length | 1,840 m. It starts on land near Foreshore Pde / Railway House (115.345818, -33.645238) and ends at the observatory (115.338603, -33.630114) | [OSM] | way 174700571 |
| OSM pier polygon (`man_made=pier`) | Covers railway chainage **s = 131 m to 1,840 m** (about 1,710 m over sand and water) | [OSM] | way 60231680 |
| Typical deck width | **About 4.5 to 5.2 m** on the long run (s = 400 to 1,750). Wider spots: s ≈ 150 to 350 (Interpretive Centre deck, about 10 to 20 m), s ≈ 700 (about 8.6 m), s ≈ 1,600 (about 8.8 m, a widened bay), and the end platform (s ≈ 1,800, about 12 m, matching inHerit's 12 m max) | [OSM] (outline is hand-traced, ±1 m) | Measured perpendicular to the railway every 50 m. |
| Deck height above water | Not published. Articles say only "high above the surface". **About 3 to 4 m above mean sea level** at the outer end | [G] | [WeekendNotes](https://www.weekendnotes.com/busselton-jetty/42797/). Busselton tide range is small (about 0.5 to 0.9 m). |
| Piles | Timber (jarrah originally). The 2011 refurbishment added a mix of timber, steel and concrete: about **200 new timber piles and 108 steel piles**, mostly in the rebuilt **900 m** section | [C] | [Xypex case study (PDF)](https://da.xypex.com/uploads/Projects/44685/Xypex%20-%20Busselton%20Jetty.pdf), [inHerit](https://inherit.dplh.wa.gov.au/Public/Inventory/PrintSingleRecord/b3184242-645a-4fb3-9650-3e5ccc2f6e5a) |
| Pile layout | Not published. Typical WA timber jetty: bents (cross-rows) of **3 to 4 piles** at **about 4.5 to 6 m** spacing, with a timber headstock on each bent, stringers along, and deck planks across | [G] | The older seaward sections look denser and more irregular, with heavy marine growth: "older piles have very little bare timber showing" [C, Landscope]. |
| Pile look | Below the waterline, thickly covered in soft corals, sponges and sea squirts (orange, yellow, red). Above it, grey weathered timber | [C] | [Landscope](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080052/080052-17.053.pdf), [Caravan World](https://caravanworld.com.au/blogs/wa-caravan-and-touring-locations/walking-on-water-in-busselton-wa) |
| Deck surface | Timber planks across the jetty (kept for heritage in the rebuilt sections). Grey and weathered, they make a "plonking" sound underfoot | [C] | Xypex PDF, Caravan World |
| Railing | **One side has no handrail, as in the original 1865 build. The other side carries a run of memorial plaques.** New timber balustrades were added in the refurbishment | [C] (wording); which side is which is [G] | [WeekendNotes](https://www.weekendnotes.com/busselton-jetty/42797/), inHerit history. My recollection: a timber post-and-rail balustrade about 1.0 to 1.1 m high on the walkway (west) side, and a low timber kerb or no rail on the train (east) side. Check against photos. |
| Bends | Long straight from the shore heading **327°** (NNW) to s ≈ 640 m. Then two left kinks, **-8.7° at s ≈ 640** (115.341950, -33.640537) and **-10.2° at s ≈ 757** (115.341438, -33.639579), to a heading of about **346°** for the long outer straight (about 1 km). A small S-wiggle in the last 80 m (s ≈ 1,763 to 1,801) into the end platform | [OSM] | From the railway way's vertices. The shore end (s = 0 to 150) curves on land from about 290° round to 327°. |
| History affecting the look | The 1978 Cyclone Alby wrecked the shore end. A fire in Dec 1999 burnt 65 m about 200 m from the end. The A$27 m refurbishment of about half the structure was finished in 2011. A 13-sculpture underwater reef has been in place since 2023 | [C] | Wikipedia, Landscope |
